Road Safety Education Pack 
Welcome to the EASST Education Pack.  This pack is designed to help teach road safety 
in a variety of formal and informal education settings. 
By using the simple, fun, educational activities, teachers and parents can help young 
people stay safe on the roads.  
This pack provides the resources you need to teach children and young people basic road 
safety principles in an age-appropriate way.  
The key principles underlying all the activities in this pack are: 
x Awareness of traffic and its dangers. 
x Good behaviour around roads. 
x Making safe choices to keep you and others safe. 
Why Teach Road Safety? 
On average more than 500 under 18’s die on the world's roads each day. Road crashes 
are the biggest killer of young people in every world region.  
Road safety is vital for children and young people. It is never too early to teach them about 
basic safety skills. More than 1.2 million people die on the world's roads every year and 
tens of millions of people are injured or disabled. 
Children are particularly at risk because: 
x They are smaller so find it harder to see, and be seen by, drivers. 
x They are less able to recognise dangerous situations and lack the maturity to make 
good decisions about safe behaviour. 
x They are physically smaller and so are more likely to be hurt by an impact. 
Educators can protect children by teaching life-saving messages and skills.  Making young 
people aware of the risks and providing them with strategies to remain safe are important 
parts of their development. 
Road Safety is a great subject for engaging children and young people. Even the youngest 
child will know something about it, because everyone uses roads and road safety impacts 
everyone.

Road Safety Education Pack (cont.) 
Introducing the Education Pack 
This pack provides stimulating and engaging activities to introduce road safety messages 
and behaviours. 
The pack is split into three sections: 
x Under 6s 
x 6 – 11 year olds 
x 12– 14 year olds  
The topics in each section vary according to the target age group, but they all relate to our 
key principles. 
How to Use the Education Pack 
Each section of the pack provides age-appropriate resources, along with introductory 
notes and advice for teachers and group leaders. 
These background notes and resources give all the information you need to run road 
safety sessions with young people. 
The resources consist of a mixture of teacher-led ACTIVITIES (lesson plans and 
exercises), as well as HANDOUTS designed to be copied and given to the children. 
The activities and handouts form a road safety education programme that can be 
incorporated into lessons or after-school activities. We suggest having different activities 
each week over a number of weeks, and reinforcing road safety messages whenever 
possible.  
The activities and handouts can be used individually, too, to raise specific road safety 
issues. Each one is a stand-alone exercise for improving safety awareness. 
When using these resources, please remember the following points: 
Key Messages 
The Teachers’ notes for each section outline the key messages for the age group.  Each 
exercise also includes key message and objectives. Try to reinforce these key messages 
at all times.   
Active Learning 
Children and young people learn best when active and engaged. Try to make your 
sessions as interactive and hands-on as possible.

Road Safety Education Pack (cont.) 
Real Life Experience 
It is essential for children and young people to learn about roads, traffic and safe 
behaviour at first hand. Always try to include outdoor practical activities in your road 
safety teaching. 
Understanding the Vocabulary 
For children to engage fully and understand road safety, they need to be familiar with the 
relevant vocabulary.  Make sure the young people you are working with understand the 
terms you are using.  This is particularly important with young children but remains 
relevant for all age groups. 
Diversity 
This pack is designed to focus on universally applicable road safety messages and 
behaviours to keep children safe.  However, the children in your group may have different 
experiences of roads and traffic and you may need to adapt your activities accordingly. 
For example, some children will come from homes where there is no car, or they might live 
on a street with no pavement.  In addition, children in urban and rural areas will face 
different issues, and their sessions may require a different emphasis. 
Sensitivities 
Group leaders should always check whether there is any child in the group with a family 
member or friend that has been involved in a serious traffic incident. Special sensitivity will 
need to be shown in these cases, and it may be appropriate to pre-warn the child’s parent 
or carer so they are able to prepare the child for the activities. 
Involving Parents / Carers 
In addition to teaching the children, it is vital to include and involve parents with your road 
safety teaching. Children will be influenced by the behaviour of their parents.  This pack 
includes a letter you can give to parents that makes them aware of what the children have 
learned and how they can reinforce these messages.

UNDER 6s – Introductory Notes  
This section of the pack teaches the following key messages: 
Traffic is dangerous and can hurt people. 
Never go near roads without a grown up and always hold their hand. 
Stop, Look and Listen before crossing the road. 
Be Bright – so that drivers can see you. 
Always wear a seat belt. 
Always get in and out of a car safely. 
 
 
This section introduces basic road safety messages to children under 6 years old.  It 
focuses on awareness of the dangers of traffic, introducing young children to the issues 
and helping them explore the ideas raised.   
It is essential for young children to learn about roads, traffic and safe behaviour at first 
hand. One of the activities in this section involves taking children outside.  Always 
remember that good planning and close supervision are both vital when taking young 
children near traffic. 
Getting Started  
The Under 6s section starts with two activities and a handout to introduce the key issues. 
 
ACTIVITY: ‘Let’s Look at a Car!’ is an outside activity to introduce young children to the 
dangers of traffic.  Before starting the exercise you should have a short group discussion 
with the children and explain that you will be looking at road safety and the dangers of 
traffic.  
 
ACTIVITY: ‘Create a Giant Road Map’ is a craft activity to build on the children’s 
knowledge of traffic dangers. It should teach the children that roads are for traffic and 
pavements are for pedestrians.  This activity may require some pre-planning to ensure you 
have the resources you need.   
 
HANDOUT: ‘Play Away From Traffic’ is a colouring sheet that can be used to reinforce 
the learning from the first two activities.

Under 6s – Introductory Notes  
Always Hold Hands 
The next two exercises teach children always to hold hands with an adult when near a 
road.  This is a simple but essential message. Children under 8 do not have the capacity to 
judge distance or speed accurately, so it is vital that they understand never to cross a road 
without an adult. 
 
ACTIVITY: ‘Hand Print Poster’ is a craft activity that gets each child to engage with the 
message as well as creating a bright fun poster to display and to remind the children of the 
importance of holding hands. 
 
HANDOUT: ‘Always Hold Hands’ is a colouring activity to reinforce this message. 
Stop, Look and Listen 
Although children at this age should never cross a road alone, it is not too early to 
introduce the rules for crossing a road safely.  The next few exercises introduce the 
concept of ‘Stop, look and listen’.   
 
HANDOUT: ‘Stop, Look and Listen’ is an information sheet showing the five stages of 
crossing the road simply and clearly.  It is designed for trainers to use when introducing 
the concept to children for the first time.   
 
HANDOUT: ‘Crossing the Road’ – This ‘Stop, Look and Listen’ colouring sheet can be 
used to reinforce the message. 
 
ACTIVITY: The Stop, Look and Listen Action Game is an interactive game to help 
children remember the key points. 
Be Bright 
The next two exercises help teach children the importance of bright clothes to ensure they 
are seen in poor light or weather. 
 
ACTIVITY: ‘Bright is Right’ is a story to read to the children to introduce these issues.  
The follow up questions will help you to start a structured discussion about being seen. 
 
HANDOUT: ‘Colour Me Bright’ is a colouring activity that reinforces this message.

ACTIVITY: Let’s Look at a Car!1 
Teacher’s Notes: This is a teacher-led activity. These notes are for the group leader and 
are not designed to be given to the children. 
Time Needed: Up to 45 minutes - depending on the number of children in the group. 
Key Messages: Traffic is dangerous and can hurt people.  Never go near roads without a 
grown up and always hold their hand. 
Objective: To introduce young children to the risks of traffic and understand how to stay 
safe. 
 
For this activity, the group leader will need to park a car in a safe off-road area.  The car 
should be parked away from traffic somewhere where the children can approach it safely. 
The car should be on a flat surface with the handbrake on and the engine off.  
 
The group leader should explain to the children the importance of standing well away from 
cars, even when they look like they are not moving.  
 
Take each child up to the side of the car in turn, holding their hand. Ask the children to poke 
the car and then poke their tummy. Which is soft? Which is hard? Explain that cars are hard 
and can hurt you if they hit you. You are soft and easily hurt.  
 
Get the children to look at the tyre. Draw their attention to how big and hard it is. Explain 
that it goes round very fast. 
 
Teach the children that cars and other vehicles are not toys. They are dangerous.  
 
Remind them to stay away from traffic and only go near a road when holding an adult’s 
hand.

ACTIVITY: Create a Giant Road Map2 
Teacher’s Notes: This is a teacher led activity. These notes are for the group leader and 
are not designed to be given to the children. 
Time Needed: Up to 1 hour.  
Key Messages: Traffic is dangerous and can hurt people.  Never go near roads without a 
grown up and always hold their hand. 
Objective: This is a creative activity to encourage children to discuss road safety and 
recognise that roads are for traffic and pavements are for pedestrians.   
 
This activity encourages children to make a giant map of roads and pavements out of 
coloured paper stuck together.  
 
Either in advance of the activity, or as part of it, create a large background sheet with a 
clearly marked road and pavement.  You could include features from your local area, like a 
park or school.  If not all the roads in your area have pavements, make sure the 
background sheet reflects your local conditions and talk to the children about how to keep 
safe.  Explain to the children about the importance of keeping to the edge of the road if 
there is no dedicated pedestrian space. 
 
Help the children cut out pictures of vehicles, people, dogs and buggies out of old 
magazines.  (If you do not have a supply of magazines, you could print pictures from the 
internet in advance). 
 
Ask the children to glue the pictures in the appropriate places on your giant map. In other 
words, vehicles on the road, people on the pavement or a suitable safe space away from 
the traffic, and so on. 
 
Whilst the children are sticking the pictures, talk to them about staying safe around roads 
using key words and phrases including ‘road’, ‘traffic’, ‘pavement’, ‘pedestrian’ and ‘holding 
hands’.  
 
When you have finished your giant road map, attach it to a wall as part of a road safety 
display. At the end of the session, get the children to show the map to their parents / 
carers.

ACTIVITY: The Stop, Look and Listen Game  
Teacher’s Notes: This is a teacher led activity. These notes are for the group leader and 
are not designed to be given to the children. 
Time Needed: 30 minutes.  
Key Message: Stop, Look and Listen before crossing the road. 
Objective: To introduce the ‘Stop, Look and Listen’ message to children.  
 
Talk to the children about the dangers when crossing the road. Explain that before 
crossing, they should always hold an adult’s hand and should ‘Stop, Look and Listen’ for 
traffic. 
Once they have understood the message you can explain the rules of the ‘Stop, Look and 
Listen’ action game. 
The Instructions 
The children should dance around the room to music.  After a few moments, stop the 
music and call out an action - the children must make the appropriate movement.  The aim 
is not to be the last child. However, there is no penalty for being last. 
The actions 
Hold hands: Each child must hold hands with the nearest person. 
Stop: 
 The children should stand still. 
Look:   
 The children should raise their hands to their forehead (as if shielding their  
 
 eyes from the sun to see better) and look around in all directions. 
Listen: 
 The children should be very quiet and cup their hands behind their ears as if  
 
 listening hard.

ACTIVITY: Story Time: Bright is Right3 
Teacher’s notes: This is a teacher led activity. These notes are for the group leader and 
are not designed to be given to the children. 
Time Needed: 30 minutes. 
Key Message: Be Bright – so that drivers can see you 
Objective: To read the story to the children and discuss the issues raised in the Follow Up

It was the day before the party at Amy’s friend’s house. 
Amy was excited. ‘Dad, can I wear my new black party dress?’ asked Amy. ‘And can we 
walk there?’  
‘Well, now that you are four I think that’s OK,’ said her dad. ‘It is not far. Just down the 
road’  
‘Whoopee!’ Amy danced about. She loved her new dress.  
The next evening was the night of the party. She put on her new black dress and black 
shoes. 
‘You look very smart’ said dad, smiling. ‘But you have to wear your reflective armband.’  
He held up two white bands. ‘But that will spoil my outfit’ said Amy, pulling a face. ‘It’s not 
smart.’ ‘But it will keep you safe,’ said Dad. ‘It helps drivers to see you at night.’ 
Dad put on his band and Amy did too, but she wished she could make it disappear. It was 
very dark outside and cold. Amy was excited, but she held on to her dad’s hand.  
Amy heard some children laughing up ahead. It was hard to see in the dark but they 
sounded like her friends, Sam and Peter. Then she saw them. They were dressed in smart 
trousers and dark coats. Amy called to them and they waited for her on the pavement.  
Suddenly, Dad shouted, ‘Watch out!’  
A car was reversing out of a driveway towards Sam and Peter. Their mum saw it just in 
time and pulled the boys out of the way.

ACTIVITY: Story Time: Bright is Right (Cont.) 
The car driver stopped and opened his windows. ‘You nearly hit us,’ said the boys’ mum.

Follow Up Activities 
Once you have read the story to the children, discuss the following questions: 
 
x Why was Amy dressed in black?  
 
x Where was she going? 
 
x What happened to Peter and Sam?  
 
x Why did the car nearly hit them?  
 
x Why did Amy wear bright clothes the next day? 
 
Talk to the children about what they are wearing now. Who is wearing bright clothes?  
Who is wearing dark clothes?   
Talk about how bright clothes can help drivers to see you when you are near traffic and 
how it can keep children safe. Which child would be easiest for drivers to see? 
Emphasise that it is sometimes difficult for drivers to see small children even during the 
day, especially when the weather is bad.

ACTIVITY: Getting In and Out of the Car  
Teacher’s notes: This is a teacher led activity. These notes are for the group leader and 
are not designed to be given to the children. 
Time Needed: 20 minutes.  
Key message: Always wear a seat belt. 
Objective: This activity teaches children only to get in or out of the car on the pavement 
side and on the side away from traffic. 
 
Explain to the children about the dangers when getting in and out of a car, and why they 
must never get out on the road side and only open the door on the side away from the 
traffic.   
Talk to them about their experiences of getting in and out of cars.   
Role Play  
Use this role play activity to help the children practise getting in and out of a car safely.   
Put two chairs side by side and two chairs behind them – as if they were seats in a car.  
Use tape, chalk or other markers on the floor to mark out the pavement.   
Encourage the children to sit in the car, as if they were passengers.  Allow the children to 
practise getting out of the car – ensure that they always leave on the pavement side.   
You can also use this exercise to talk about other relevant issues such as: 
x The importance of wearing seat belts. 
x Staying on the pavement. 
x Waiting for an adult once of out the car. 
x Always holding an adult’s hand near the road. 
x Wearing bright clothes so that you can be seen by drivers once out of the car.

ACTIVITY: The Walk Safer Game3 
Teacher’s Notes: This is a teacher led activity. These notes are for the group leader and

Time Needed: 15 minutes.  
Key Messages: Safe behaviour around roads and ‘Stop, Look and Listen’. 
Objective: Use this activity to reinforce the messages that you have discussed in earlier

This is an action game.  You will need to teach the children a set of actions they must do 
when instructions are called out in a random order. Children need to spread out in a hall 
or room. The instructions and actions are as follows: 
• Single file! [All get in a line one behind the other] 
• Hold hands! [Hold hands with nearest person] 
• Use the pavement! [Go to the side of the room indicated] 
• Stop, look and listen! [Stand still, quietly, looking all around, ready to cross] 
• Walk across! [Walk as if crossing a road, looking all around] 
To play the game, show the children the actions and let them practise each one. Then 
start to call out the instructions in random order. The idea is not to be the last child to do 
the action (but in this case no one is out). 
Start slowly and speed up as the children get more familiar with the actions.  
Finish the game by letting children rest and asking them questions such as why they 
need to walk in single file on narrow country lanes and why it is important to hold hands 
near roads.

Road Safety Education Pack 
Welcome to the EASST Education Pack.  This pack is designed to help teach road safety 
in a variety of formal and informal education settings. 
By using the simple, fun, educational activities, teachers and parents can help young 
people stay safe on the roads.  
This pack provides the resources you need to teach children and young people basic road 
safety principles in an age-appropriate way.  
The key principles underlying all the activities in this pack are: 
x Awareness of traffic and its dangers. 
x Good behaviour around roads and the rules to follow. 
x Making safe choices to keep you and others safe. 
Why Teach Road Safety? 
On average more than 500 under 18s die on the world's roads each day. Road crashes 
are the biggest killer of young people in every world region. 
Road safety is vital for children and young people. It is never too early to teach them about 
basic safety skills.  More than 1.2 million people die on the world's roads every year and 
tens of millions of people are injured or disabled. 
Children are particularly at risk because: 
x They are smaller so find it harder to see, and be seen by, drivers. 
x They are less able to recognise dangerous situations and lack the maturity to make 
good decisions about safe behaviour.

focused on the following messages: 
 
Be alert and always look for hazards. 
Don’t take risks around roads. 
Road safety is for everyone. 
Avoid peer pressure. 
Always wear a seat belt. 
 
As young people get older, they have more independence than small children.  In addition, 
teenagers have a tendency to take more risks, overestimate their abilities and be 
influenced by their peers to push the boundaries of safe behaviour.  As a result, this age 
group is one of those at the highest risk on roads.   
This project gets young people to appreciate the importance of good behaviour around 
roads, increase their ability to assess hazards and helps them to recognise and resist peer 
pressure. 
As a parent you are a role model for your child.  Please reinforce these road safety 
messages at every opportunity by setting a good example through your own actions.  
Seeing good behaviour on the road and in the car will help your child learn safety skills 
and apply these throughout their lives.
